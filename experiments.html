Fanuc M-710iC/70: Joint Articulation & Kinematics

Exploring joint degrees of freedom, limits, and multi-axis articulation on an industrial Fanuc M-710iC/70 manipulator inside NVIDIA Isaac Sim. Verifying drive damping, velocity limits, and reachable workspace geometry before wiring high-level planners or training policies.

Hypothesis & Goal
Establish Ground Truth Physics

Industrial robotic arms have massive inertia and strict joint torque envelopes. Before testing any automated trajectory or reinforcement learning policy, the robot's URDF/USD physics representation must accurately model joint stiffness, damping, and mechanical limits.

Simulation Setup
Isaac Sim USD Stage

Imported Fanuc M-710iC/70 USD with complete articulation hierarchy. Actuated joints across their dynamic ranges (J1 ±180°, J2 -60°/+75°, J3 -170°/+230°, J4 ±360°, J5 ±125°, J6 ±360°) to inspect physics solver stability under fast direction reversals.

Findings & Takeaway
Smooth Kinematic Envelope

The joint articulation proved completely stable under PhysX 5 GPU stepping with zero contact tunneling or joint explosions. Revealed that wrist joints (J4–J6) require aggressive velocity damping to prevent oscillatory overshoot during rapid orientation corrections.

Isaac Lab RL Cube Pick: Policy Exploration & Failure Analysis

A mini demo attempting to use end-to-end Reinforcement Learning via NVIDIA Isaac Lab to teach the Fanuc manipulator to locate, reach, grasp, and lift a target cube. The experiment failed to converge on clean pickups — and documenting why failure happens in robotics RL is just as valuable as celebrating converged runs.

The Objective
Autonomous Cube Acquisition

Train a neural policy from scratch to guide the Fanuc arm's end-effector toward a randomly spawned cube, align parallel jaws, clamp with sufficient frictional force, and elevate the cube 15 cm above the worktable.

Failure Pathology #1
The Joint-Space Velocity Trap

Directly outputting raw 6-DoF joint velocities without an Operational Space Controller (OSC) created severe trajectory jitter. The policy learned to aggressively whip joint 1 and 2 to minimize Euclidean distance to the cube, arriving at the table with excessive downward momentum and poor gripper alignment.

Failure Pathology #2
Frictional Contact & Grasp Squeeze

Without tactile pressure sensors or fine contact compliance in the reward formulation, the gripper closed either too early (knocking the cube away like a billiard ball) or closed with uneven pad contact, ejecting the cube out of the jaws upon lift-off.

Engineering Takeaway
Why Hybrid Control Wins

Heavy industrial robots (70 kg payload class) shouldn't be controlled end-to-end via unconstrained joint RL for simple pick tasks. The optimal architecture is classical motion planning (RMPflow / MoveIt) for the macro approach, with RL or impedance control restricted to the fine 10 cm micro-grasp phase.

Enterprise AI Gateway: LiteLLM, Governance, & Team Budgets

When companies ask "How do we start implementing AI into workflows and using agents?", the real question is never just which model to pick — it is how to govern it, apply privacy guardrails, allocate departmental budgets, and ensure zero-downtime fallbacks. Tinkering with LiteLLM to build an enterprise proxy: provisioning scoped virtual keys, setting spending caps, enforcing prompt guardrails before data leaves the network, and establishing a unified gateway for multi-agent frameworks like LangGraph.

The Core Problem
The "Free Service" Privacy Trap

Workers rapidly adopt consumer AI accounts for everyday tasks. But if a service is free, your internal data is the product. Unchecked copy-pasting of customer records or factory logs leaks proprietary data into external training stores. LiteLLM acts as a sovereign checkpoint: defining company policy, intercepting data, and logging all usage before prompts ever touch an LLM.

Cost Governance
Teams, Quotas, & Virtual Keys

Rather than handing out unmetered root API keys, the gateway provisions isolated Virtual Keys with strict Rate-Per-Minute (RPM) and Token-Per-Minute (TPM) ceilings. Each department receives its own budget dashboard (as configured in the teams view), isolating financial risk and preventing runaway recursive agent loops from draining capital.

High Availability
Dynamic Fallbacks & Smart Routing

If a primary model provider experiences an outage, 503 error, or token rate-limit cliff, LiteLLM's configured fallback chain automatically redirects requests to a secondary model without downstream disruption. Routine tasks route to fast, low-cost models; complex reasoning routes to heavyweight models seamlessly.

Agent Ready
LangGraph & Tool Support

Modern autonomous systems (like customer support agents built in LangGraph or event-driven tools) require standard OpenAI-compatible endpoints with high reliability. The gateway provides unified authorization, memory connection points, and structured audit logs so every tool call and agent decision is observable and reproducible.
